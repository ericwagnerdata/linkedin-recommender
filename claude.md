## LinkedIn Recommender Writing Rules

### Tone
- Write like a respected colleague, not HR. Recommendations should feel personal and specific.
- Never start with filler openers like "I had the pleasure of..." or "It is my honor to..." -- they waste the first impression.
- Avoid superlative stacking. Don't use "exceptional, outstanding, brilliant" together. Pick one and back it with evidence.

### Structure
- Every recommendation needs at least one concrete, specific example. A real situation beats a list of traits.
- Follow a 3-paragraph structure: (1) relationship and context, (2) specific accomplishment with evidence, (3) forward-looking endorsement.
- Target length is 100-150 words. 125 is ideal. Over 200 is almost always too long.

### Persona and Relationship
- Match the writer's voice. A VP writes differently than a peer, direct report, or client.
- Make the relationship unmistakable in the first sentence. Vague relationships undermine credibility.

### Output Quality
- Never use em dashes (--). Replace with a comma, period, or restructure the sentence.
- Use role-specific terminology from the subject's actual field. Avoid generic business language like "synergy" or "leverage."
- End with a specific, memorable claim -- not a platitude like "I recommend her without hesitation."
